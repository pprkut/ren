A year at the lotus pond

Marta Visser · 27 November 2026 · 4 min read

A year ago we sank a thermometer on a string into the lotus pond behind the field station and promised ourselves to read it every week. We missed three weeks in February, when the pond was more ice than water, and one in July, when all of us were away at once. The rest is below.

When the leaves rise

The first leaves broke the surface in the second week of May, when the water had stayed above 15 °C for ten days in a row. They floated flat at first; the tall leaves, held high above the water on their stalks, came a month later. By the end of June the eastern half of the pond was covered, and the moorhens had moved to the western half.

The flowers open in the morning and close again in the afternoon, for three days. On the third day they don't close any more, and the petals fall.

We counted 41 flowers over the summer, against 26 the year before. Whether that is the warm June or the mud we cleared in March, a single year can't tell.
